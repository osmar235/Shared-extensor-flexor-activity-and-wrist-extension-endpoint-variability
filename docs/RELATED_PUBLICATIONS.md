# Related research and citation relevance

The following publications provide context for the manuscript. Include a citation in the manuscript only where the cited study directly supports the specific statement. These publications do not demonstrate the mechanism responsible for the present ECU-FCR association.

1. Neto, O. P., Baweja, H. S., & Christou, E. A. (2010). Increased voluntary drive is associated with changes in common oscillations from 13 to 60 Hz of interference but not rectified electromyography. *Muscle & Nerve*, 42(3), 348–354. https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.21687
   - Relevant to the role of EMG rectification and sensitivity of interference EMG to oscillatory input.
2. Pinto Neto, O., & Christou, E. A. (2010). Rectification of the EMG signal impairs the identification of oscillatory input to the muscle. *Journal of Neurophysiology*, 103(2), 1093–1103. https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00792.2009
   - Relevant to methodological cautions when measuring oscillatory EMG coupling. Already cited in the manuscript.
3. Wang, Y., Pinto Neto, O., Weinrich, M. M., Castro, R., Wright, T., & Kennedy, D. M. (2022). The influence of distal and proximal muscle activation on neural crosstalk. *PLOS ONE*, 17(10), e0275997. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0275997
   - Relevant as an example combining EMG coherence and motor performance in a different, **bimanual** task; its authors' neural-crosstalk interpretation is not direct evidence of electrical crosstalk or neural drive in the present unilateral recordings.
4. Marchini, A., Pereira, R., Pedroso, W., Christou, E., & Pinto Neto, O. (2017). Age-associated differences in motor output variability and coordination during the simultaneous dorsiflexion of both feet. *Somatosensory & Motor Research*, 34(2), 96–101. https://doi.org/10.1080/08990220.2017.1313220
   - Potentially relevant to distinctions between motor-output variability and coordination across people, but not direct evidence about ECU-FCR endpoint dispersion.
5. Pinto Neto, O., Pinho, T. O. R., Wang, Y., Balbinot, G., & Kennedy, D. M. (2026 online; 2027 volume issue). A computational framework for EMG simulation and coherence-based biomarker analysis on neural crosstalk in bimanual coordination. *Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine*, 288, 109657. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2026.109657
   - Relevant for the separation of modeled common neural input from signal-level coherence. This is a computational model of **bimanual** coupling, not validation of the present unilateral ECU-FCR mechanism.

6. Pinto Neto, O., Weinrich, M., Abbott, R., Keller, N., Rao, R., Wright, T., Dunbar, B. J., Diaz-Artiles, A., & Kennedy, D. M. (2026). Reduced gravity weakens muscle activation coupling and destabilizes bimanual force control. *Journal of Neurophysiology*, 135(5), 1246–1259. https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00632.2025
   - Particularly relevant to the coherence–behavior interpretation: EMG-EMG coherence and motor-output variability covaried under altered gravitational constraints, without establishing a universal direction of association. This was a **bimanual** task and should not be presented as a direct replication of unilateral ECU–FCR findings.
7. Lima, V. M. S., Arthur, E. F., Gonzaga, R. R. D., Diniz, L. F., Pedreiro, R. C. M., & Pinto Neto, O. (2026). Effects of transcranial direct current stimulation over the left sensorimotor cortex on bimanual force control: A computational and experimental investigation. *Bioengineering*, 13(5), 502. https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering13050502
   - Potential supporting context about task-specific motor-control mechanisms; include only if discussing differences between cortical-level interventions and the present non-invasive muscle recordings.
